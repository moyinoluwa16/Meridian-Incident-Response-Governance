Meridian Health Network – Incident Response Program Governance
This project examines incident response governance at Meridian Health Network following a simulated cybersecurity incident involving ransomware, a phishing campaign, and suspicious account activity. It assesses incident severity, establishes response responsibilities through a RACI matrix, and outlines internal notification procedures and HIPAA breach notification considerations.

Project Overview
Meridian Health Network is facing three related security incidents affecting its clinic operations and potentially exposing sensitive patient information. The purpose of this assignment is to assess the incidents, determine their severity, define incident response responsibilities, and establish appropriate internal notification and regulatory assessment procedures.

The three identified security incidents are:
1. Ransomware Attack: File shares at two clinics have been encrypted, disrupting access to important files and potentially affecting patient care.
2. Phishing Campaign: An email impersonating IT Support may be an attempt to steal employee credentials or gain unauthorized access to the network.
3. Suspicious Account Activity: After-hours logins using a former contractor's account suggest possible unauthorized access.
Project objectives:
- Assess and classify the severity of the combined security incidents.
- Establish clear incident response responsibilities using a RACI matrix.
- Identify the internal stakeholders who must be notified during the first 30–60 minutes.
- Examine potential HIPAA breach notification obligations.
- Recommend improvements to incident response governance and preparedness.
- Establish a foundation for a formal Incident Response Plan and threat-specific response procedures.

Configuration Steps
The following steps outline the incident assessment, response governance, notification, and compliance considerations presented in the assignment.

1. Identify and Assess the Security Incidents
- Identify the ransomware attack affecting file shares at two clinics.
- Assess the phishing campaign impersonating IT Support.
- Investigate suspicious after-hours logins using a former contractor's account.
- Consider the potential impact on patient care, data confidentiality, and business operations.
2. Determine Incident Severity
- Classify the combined incidents as SEV-1 (Critical).
- Consider the disruption caused by ransomware at two clinics.
- Evaluate the possibility that the former contractor's account has been compromised.
- Consider the potential exposure of patient information.
- Escalate immediately without waiting for confirmation of data theft before initiating the response.
3. Establish Incident Response Responsibilities Using a RACI Matrix

   The RACI framework defines the responsibilities of the following stakeholders:
- Incident Commander: Coordinates the overall response and makes key incident management decisions.
- IT/Network Lead: Conducts technical investigation and containment.
- Legal and Compliance: Assesses potential privacy breaches and regulatory obligations.
- Communications Lead: Prepares and coordinates approved internal and external communications.
- Business/Clinical Leadership: Assesses patient-care disruption and coordinates clinical workarounds.
RACI Matrix
Activity	Incident Commander	IT/Network Lead	Legal & Compliance	Comms Lead	Business/Clinical Leadership
Declare incident severity and coordinate response	A/R	C	C	I	I
Investigate ransomware and suspicious logins	A	R	C	I	I
Contain affected systems and accounts	A	R	C	I	C
Assess patient-care disruption and continuity arrangements	A	C	I	I	R
Assess potential HIPAA breach and notification obligations	I	C	A/R	I	C
Prepare and approve external communications	A	C	C	R	C
Coordinate clinical workarounds and service updates	A	C	I	I	R

RACI Definitions:
- R – Responsible: Performs the activity.
- A – Accountable: Owns the final decision or outcome.
- C – Consulted: Provides advice or input.
- I – Informed: Receives updates about the activity.
4. Initiate Internal Notifications Within the First 30–60 Minutes
- IT and Security Team: Notify the team to investigate the ransomware, phishing email, and suspicious account activity, and isolate affected systems where necessary.
- Executive Sponsor or Senior Management: Inform leadership about the incident severity, operational disruption, and potential business impact.
- Clinical Leadership at Affected Clinics: Confirm the impact on patient care and coordinate temporary workarounds to maintain essential services.
- Legal and Compliance Team: Initiate assessment of potential patient information exposure and applicable regulatory obligations.
- Communications Lead: Prepare internal communications and, where necessary, customer or patient messages for approval.
- Helpdesk and Relevant Department Heads: Ensure incident reports are documented and employees receive consistent instructions.
5. Establish Emergency Communication and Documentation Procedures
- Use an approved emergency communication channel.
- Maintain records of incident response decisions.
- Document actions taken by the response team.
- Maintain an incident timeline to support investigation and accountability.
- Ensure employees receive consistent and approved instructions.
6. Assess Potential HIPAA Breach Notification Obligations
- Determine whether Protected Health Information (PHI) was involved.
- Investigate whether patient information was accessed, acquired, used, or disclosed without authorization.
- Determine whether the information was properly protected.
- Recognize that ransomware or a phishing email alone does not automatically confirm a reportable HIPAA breach.
- Have Legal and Compliance lead the breach assessment and determine applicable notification obligations.
7. Determine Applicable HIPAA Notification Requirements
- Assess whether notification to affected individuals is required.
- Determine whether notification to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is required.
- Consider other relevant parties where applicable.
- Where HIPAA breach notification is required, ensure affected individuals are notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery of the breach.
8. Strengthen Incident Response Program Governance
- Establish a formal Incident Response Plan.
- Assign clear incident response responsibilities.
- Develop threat-specific playbooks for ransomware, phishing, and suspicious account activity.
- Conduct regular tabletop exercises.
- Review incident response procedures to improve organizational preparedness and reduce the impact of future incidents.
Results and Findings
The assessment identified three related cybersecurity incidents and determined that their combined impact requires immediate escalation and coordinated response.

1. Incident Severity: SEV-1 (Critical)

The incident was classified as SEV-1 because:
- Ransomware has already encrypted file shares at two clinics.
- Access to important files and business operations has been disrupted.
- Patient care may be affected.
- Suspicious activity involving a former contractor's account suggests possible unauthorized access.
- Patient information may have been exposed.
The assessment recommends treating the incident as critical while the investigation is ongoing rather than waiting for confirmation of data theft.

2. Incident Response Governance and Accountability

The RACI matrix established clear responsibilities across five stakeholder groups.
- The Incident Commander coordinates the overall response.
- The IT/Network Lead manages technical investigation and containment.
- Legal and Compliance assess potential privacy breaches and regulatory obligations.
- The Communications Lead prepares approved communications.
- Business/Clinical Leadership manages the impact on patient care and clinic operations.
This structure supports coordinated decision-making and reduces uncertainty about incident response responsibilities.

3. Internal Notification Requirements

The assignment identified the stakeholders who should be notified within the first 30–60 minutes:
- IT and Security Team.
- Executive Sponsor or Senior Management.
- Clinical Leadership at Affected Clinics.
- Legal and Compliance Team.
- Communications Lead.
- Helpdesk and Relevant Department Heads.
The assessment also emphasizes the importance of approved emergency communication channels, consistent employee instructions, and accurate incident documentation.

4. HIPAA Breach Notification Considerations

The incident could trigger mandatory HIPAA breach notification requirements if the investigation confirms that unsecured PHI was accessed, acquired, used, or disclosed without authorization.

However, the presence of ransomware or a phishing campaign does not automatically establish that a reportable breach has occurred.

The assessment identified Legal and Compliance as the lead for determining whether patient information was involved and whether notification to affected individuals, HHS, or other relevant parties is required.

Where HIPAA notification is required, affected individuals must generally be notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery of the breach.

5. Recommendations for Improvement

Meridian Health Network should:
- Establish a formal Incident Response Plan.
- Define clear incident response roles and responsibilities.
- Develop threat-specific playbooks for ransomware, phishing, and suspicious account activity.
- Establish internal escalation and notification procedures.
- Maintain incident timelines, decisions, and response documentation.
- Ensure clinical continuity arrangements are considered during security incidents.
- Conduct regular tabletop exercises to test incident response readiness.
Overall Conclusion

The project demonstrates the importance of incident response governance in managing cybersecurity incidents that can disrupt healthcare operations and threaten sensitive patient information.

By establishing a formal Incident Response Plan, assigning clear responsibilities, strengthening internal notification procedures, and conducting regular preparedness exercises, Meridian Health Network can improve its ability to respond to security incidents, protect patient information, maintain clinical operations, and meet applicable regulatory obligations.
